## 1. Explicit supersession and preservation

This version adopts [Template v1.1](../student-learning-portfolio-template-v1.1.md), which retains the nineteen-section outer order and visual base of v1.0 while explicitly revising its pedagogical semantics. It supersedes the v1.1 Portfolio contract's exclusive references to Template v1.0 and Class Report v1.0. Within Section 17, [Class Report v1.1](CLASS-REPORT-PRESENTATION-CONTRACT-V1.1_2026-09-19.md) now governs.

Other requirements remain: responsible-adult metadata for a minor/family edition; private authoring-control sections; no new mandatory Family Executive Summary inside the template; preservation of history, source evidence and teacher authorization.

## 2. What the Portfolio preserves

**Portfolio = complete confirmed lesson archive + cumulative learning memory.**

Every confirmed Class Report remains in the archive, independently of whether the lesson changes state or adds an item to a cumulative bank. Vocabulary, grammar, patterns, goals and priorities are curated cumulatively from authorized evidence. A different latest topic does not erase older valid knowledge or history.

The leading narrative explains how learning is being constructed across encounters. Current State is a useful authorized summary within that story; it is not the only reason for a lesson to matter.

## 3. State, context and language for families

- State retention is a record decision, not proof of unchanged learning. Do not require a higher classification at each review.
- State change requires scoped teacher judgment across appropriate evidence and conditions; five lessons or one recall activity do not mechanically determine it.
- Distinguish the purpose and opportunities of ongoing language use from bounded school-support tasks. Do not turn an external-subject recall task into a global learner judgment.
- Include the programme's purpose of expression and participation through English. Preserve both personal language use and documented access to other knowledge through English, including scaffolded content tasks; later content recall is not the sole measure of their value.
- Preserve what helped the learner within the story. Reduced support is one possible development, not the definition of all progress.
- Explain familiar knowledge returning, self-correction, connections and reconstruction when evidenced. Do not manufacture a success story.
- Preserve substantive limitations in natural language near relevant claims; keep technical review structures in Teacher Intelligence.

The family's reading should reveal **where the learning was, what happened, what became visible and what comes next**, without needing to decode support scales or internal state transitions.

## 4. Acceptance and rollout

A compliant edition preserves the nineteen-section architecture, confirmed lesson history, cumulative evidence and the narrative requirements above. All Class Reports follow the version declared by the projection. A dated, source-linked narrative remains useful even when the recorded level is the same.

Historical editions remain intact. Regeneration is versioned and teacher-reviewed. A request to leave original lesson text untouched remains binding; a new cumulative synthesis can explain continuity without rewriting that text.

External templates, PDFs, prompt implementations and live dashboards require their own verified updates. Merging this contract alone does not make them compliant. See the [amendment registry](../governance/LEARNING-JOURNEY-AMENDMENT-2026-09-19.md).
