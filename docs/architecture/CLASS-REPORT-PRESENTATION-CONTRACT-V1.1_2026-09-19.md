## 1. Explicit supersession

Upon adoption, this version supersedes v1.0 wherever that contract requires ten separately visible numbered sections, a correction table as the primary correction story, standalone support classifications, or technical empty-state language in the student/family copy. It replaces the affected rendering and compliance rules in v1.0 sections 2, 3, 7, 8, 9 and 10.

The ten semantic obligations CR-01–CR-10 remain. Source integrity, attendance rules, no automatic CEFR movement, preservation of history and teacher authority remain mandatory. The v1.0 Markdown and JSON are historical versions, not silently rewritten definitions of v1.1. The v1.0 JSON validator cannot certify this revision.

## 2. Purpose

A Class Report explains the lesson's contribution to an ongoing learning journey. It preserves the event and its context even when no learner-state change is justified. It is a projection of authorized evidence, not a transcript dump or a new source of canonical authority.

The leading account must show the purpose, what the learner encountered or did, the supported pedagogical meaning and a useful continuation. Its value does not depend on a new achievement, new vocabulary, reduced support or a level change.

## 3. Complete evidence, translated presentation

| Obligation | Required semantic record | Student/family presentation |
|---|---|---|
| CR-01 — Identity | Lesson identity/date, authorized attendance, focus and source references | Lesson/date and a clear focus; operational metadata stays internal. |
| CR-02 — Work | Task, purpose, language/content and materials where known | What we worked on, placed in the learning sequence. |
| CR-03 — Evidence | Observable events distinct from teacher interpretation, with claim limits | What we noticed: meaningful use, return, connection or adjustment where evidenced. |
| CR-04 — Language adjustment | Original attempt, subsequent form, cue/model and point where verified | Language we worked on: explain the sequence. A concise comparison remains available when useful, without making errors the main story. |
| CR-05 — Vocabulary | Relevant terms, function, example/context and encounters | Words to use again; distinguish an observed learner utterance from a practice model. |
| CR-06 — Grammar | Relevant structure, task evidence and bounded interpretation | Patterns to try again, including familiar structures revisited. |
| CR-07 — Support/context | Independent / One clue / Model when actually observed, plus relevant task conditions; unknown when not recorded | Explain what helped within the episode. A standalone support-level ranking is not mandatory. |
| CR-08 — Teacher meaning | Authorized takeaway and current pedagogical priority | Why this episode matters and the teacher's direction. |
| CR-09 — Continuation | Specific next action, appropriate to purpose and supported need | A short action, with a model where useful; proposals are not completed homework. |
| CR-10 — Boundaries | Missing evidence, unverified results and limits on inference | Include any qualification needed to prevent a misleading claim; keep technical audit details in Teacher Intelligence. |

All ten obligations must remain traceable in the complete teacher/audit record. An obligation with no evidence must record that absence explicitly. The student copy may group obligations into coherent prose; it must not erase substantive uncertainty by moving it out of view.

## 4. Stable learner-facing sequence

Use a consistent sequence inside each report:

1. **Our lesson** — identity and purpose.
2. **What we worked on** — the task and connection to prior learning, if known.
3. **What we noticed** — source-grounded events and teacher interpretation, including support/limits where material.
4. **Language to use again** — useful words, patterns and adjustments with short examples.
5. **What comes next** — a manageable action and a reason for it.

Boundaries belong next to the claims they qualify. The sections do not require equal space, a new achievement or new language every time. Age-appropriate headings may translate these functions while keeping the same order and meaning.

## 5. Empty evidence and familiar learning

Do not equate no new bank item with no useful learning. If familiar words or structures were practised, describe that work. If they were not documented, do not invent a rehearsal merely to fill a section.

When no specific language example is documented, a plain statement such as "Specific language examples from this lesson are still to be confirmed" can preserve the gap without claiming learner failure. Do not expose `persisted`, `promoted to long-term memory` or internal evidence-state mechanics as family-facing prose.

When no verifiable correction occurred, no learner error is invented. The evidence record explicitly says so; the family narrative focuses on the documented work. Vocabulary and grammar remain distinguishable within Language to use again.

## 6. Contextual interpretation

Apply the Learning Machine's purpose/opportunity rule before any comparison. A short exam-preparation episode is not automatically a longitudinal benchmark for subject mastery or English development. Returning to an earlier topic may show partial recall or reconstruction under particular conditions; neither warrants a global label.

For a content task conducted through English, identify both the content objective and the language's role. Describe what the learner could access, say or do in that task, including useful scaffolding. Teacher-validated participation can be a meaningful lesson contribution even when long-term content retention was neither demonstrated nor the only objective. Do not treat English-mediated task participation, personal language appropriation and subject understanding as interchangeable outcomes.

Preserve attempt → opportunity/cue → adjustment when known. Do not turn a teacher model into spontaneous self-correction. Less support can be relevant evidence in comparable tasks, but is not the sole definition of progress.

## 7. Versioning and acceptance

Existing historical reports remain accessible. Any re-expression creates a new projection version with source lineage; it does not silently overwrite the earlier report or claim a new lesson. User instructions to preserve specified original lesson text remain binding.

A v1.1 report passes only when:

- CR-01–CR-10 are accounted for, with explicit internal absences where needed;
- the visible narrative explains the documented lesson contribution without requiring a state delta;
- observation, interpretation, practice models and subject-content claims are not conflated;
- comparison accounts for known purpose, opportunity, support and missing context;
- meaningful boundaries stay with claims that need them;
- no unsupported progress, mastery, forgetting, motivation or CEFR conclusion is introduced;
- teacher authorization, lineage and historical continuity are preserved.

Missing authorization or unsupported claims block publication. A missing state change does not. A thin evidence record triggers clarification or a bounded factual account, not an invented learning outcome.
