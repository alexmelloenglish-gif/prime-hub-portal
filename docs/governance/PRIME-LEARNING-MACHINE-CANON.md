# PRIME Learning Machine Canon — Non-Destructive Learning Narrative

**Version:** 1.0
**Classification:** CANONICAL CORE / derived operational rule
**Adoption:** Proposed alongside the [System Governance Canon](PRIME-SYSTEM-GOVERNANCE-CANON.md); not a claim of runtime enforcement.
**Scope:** Extraction, teacher review, authorized interpretation and all learning projections.

## 1. Governing question

For every lesson, the machine must ask:

> What happened to the learning that was already being built?

The answer may concern something encountered, practised, maintained, revisited, reconnected, recovered, self-corrected, reconstructed, extended or applied. It may concern something that needs another encounter. No new conquest or state transition is required.

`revisit / reconnect / maintain / self-correct / reconstruct / extend / apply` are interpretation principles, not required learner labels, stages, automatic success codes or a new progress taxonomy.

## 2. Three separate outputs

| Layer | Question | Authority and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Learning event | What happened, in what task and conditions? | Source-grounded observation; preserve first attempt and subsequent moves when available. |
| Pedagogical interpretation | What does this episode contribute to the journey? | AI may propose; the teacher validates meaning and limits. |
| Learner-state decision | Does the evidence justify revising a specific recorded claim? | Explicit authorized judgment; never inferred solely from the narrative or a lesson count. |

An event may exist without an interpretation, and an interpretation may be meaningful without a state update. Preserve these distinctions in storage and projections. A technical null/no-op is not a learner-facing conclusion that nothing happened.

## 3. Context before comparison

Before proposing a comparison or retrieval judgment, retain available evidence about:

- the learning target and scope: language use, subject knowledge, task performance, or a combination;
- the purpose: sustained development, school support, exam preparation, consolidation or another stated purpose;
- previous encounters and documented opportunities for practice or use;
- elapsed time and the actual prompt/task demands;
- the language used to ask and answer, and relevant materials;
- support, feedback and opportunity to reconsider;
- differences between tasks that limit comparison;
- missing context and the appropriate subject-content source.

These are semantic requirements, not an instruction to invent a new database schema in this amendment. Missing details stay unspecified. Do not infer personal relevance, motivation, practice history or understanding from task completion.

For learning through another language, distinguish the content/task objective from the language-development objective. Ask what the learner was able to access, communicate or participate in through that language, with which supports. This can be meaningful evidence of language-mediated participation without proving independent subject mastery, a CEFR change or permanent recall. Do not attribute content-task difficulty to language alone.

Where a content episode served a bounded instrumental purpose, preserve that purpose. Where language is becoming usable for personal expression across encounters, preserve those uses and the teacher's bounded interpretation. These are compatible contributions, not competing rankings of which lesson counts. Do not make later recall the only measure of the earlier task's value.

Before any broad state-change proposal, the teacher reviews the scope, repeated/comparable observations where needed, opportunities for use, support and uncertainty. There is no automatic rule that five lessons should change a level, nor that one weak response should lower a skill state.

## 4. Narrative construction

Each confirmed lesson must remain in the archive. Prepare a source-grounded draft even when the recorded level is retained, the topic is revisited, or no item is promoted into cumulative banks.

The narrative should make understandable:

**where the learning was → what happened → what became visible → where it can go next.**

Use only the parts supported by evidence. Unknown prior history is not a blank slate, and missing evidence must not be filled with an invented connection. Where learner evidence is thin, describe the known lesson purpose and activity, keep the uncertainty for teacher review, and obtain clarification before claiming an outcome.

Repetition alone does not prove consolidation. Later recall establishes that something was recalled in those conditions, not that all content is permanently retained. A correct answer does not establish independent retrieval unless that condition is documented. A supported answer may still show participation, reconstruction or connection when those events are evidenced.

## 5. Attempt, adjustment and event semantics

Retain the sequence when available: first attempt → opportunity/cue → revised response. Preserve whether a model was supplied. Attribute self-correction only when the evidence warrants it.

An activity's first-attempt score and subsequent learning have different purposes. If an exercise retains its initial score, a later adjustment can still be recorded separately. Scores must not become automatic mastery, CEFR or general-memory decisions. Do not claim that an activity preserves revisions if its tool does not record them.

The machine must treat the following as different layers:

- **Event:** what happened in a particular episode — an attempt, error, correction, self-correction, successful use, question, reconstruction or another observed occurrence.
- **Pattern:** a bounded interpretation supported by multiple relevant events under sufficiently comparable conditions.
- **State:** a separately authorized decision about a defined learner claim.

**Error is not state. Self-correction is not state. Repeated correct use is not required to produce a new state.**

A sequence such as `error → noticing → prompted correction → self-correction → repeated accurate use → less need for correction` may be a useful historical account, but it is not a required ladder. Events can recur, disappear, return or occur in another order.

Do not persist a historical support need as a permanent learner trait. For example, “needs to self-correct X” describes an observed event pattern at a given time and task context; later absence of self-correction may mean increased automaticity, no relevant opportunity, or simply no observation. The machine must not select regression or improvement without supporting evidence.

Student-generated observations can be captured as evidence events. A learner record such as **What did I say/write? → What should it be? → Can I fix it? → What do I notice?** may document noticing and reflection. It does not by itself authorize a pedagogical state change; teacher validation remains required.

## 6. Teacher review and publication

Teacher Intelligence retains source references, support conditions, corrections, uncertainty and the reasoning behind proposed connections. The teacher confirms, edits or bounds the narrative and any separate state-change proposal.

Student/family text translates authorized evidence into a human account; it does not expose the review workflow or turn internal support scales into learner identity. Use a single consolidated review where the existing workflow supports it. This amendment grants no new publishing authority.

Preserve all existing candidate → teacher decision → canonical record → verified independent projection gates. A narrative must not become a new canonical source by being well written.

## 7. Evidence absence and narrative defaults

Absence of evidence is not evidence of regression. A behavior may be absent because it was not elicited, was not relevant to the task, was not observed, or may no longer be needed in the same way.

Repeated state is not evidence of stagnation. Stability may reflect retention, consolidation, maintenance, continued use or another meaningful episode.

Do not lead student/family outputs with `unchanged`, `no progress`, `not mastered`, `failed retrieval`, `support-dependent`, `weak area` or an error list. Such language may appear in a separately governed teacher-facing analysis only with adequate scope and evidence.

Also forbidden: a forced success story, claims of transfer without evidence, a new achievement in every lesson, attributing thought processes that were not observed, or equating external-subject recall with overall language development.

## 8. Review scenarios

These are synthetic acceptance scenarios, not records of named students.

| Given | Required account | Invalid inference |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded level stays the same; familiar language is practised | Describe the task and documented reuse; retain the level as a separate fact | No learning occurred; award a new state to make the lesson valuable |
| Learner revises a verb after an opportunity to think | Preserve the observed sequence and the role of any cue | Count only the initial error; claim spontaneous self-correction when a model was given |
| A bounded exam-preparation topic returns later | Describe what was recalled, prompted or reconstructed in that context | Diagnose general memory, subject mastery or language-level regression |
| Learner participates in a content task through English with documented scaffolding | Describe the achieved participation/access and the role of support, subject to teacher validation | Treat support as cancelling the episode's value; infer independent subject mastery or lasting retention |
| Personal language use recurs across encounters | Preserve the uses and the teacher's scoped interpretation of appropriation | Infer personal ownership from one correct form; require equivalent personal relevance from all content tasks |
| Prior opportunities for reuse are unknown | Mark the comparison as limited and seek teacher context | Assume no practice, laziness or equivalent exposure |
| A familiar structure returns without new vocabulary | Retain the lesson contribution and relevant earlier memory | Erase the lesson or write a generic no-progress report |
| No classified skill state, but an authorized learning event exists | Show the event with its limits | Hide the narrative because the status is Not Assessed |
| Only activities are documented, not learner response | Describe the activities; request outcome clarification internally | Invent retention, consolidation, success or failure |
| Repeated comparable evidence supports a change | Prepare a scoped state proposal for teacher authorization | Automatically promote the learner based on points or elapsed lessons |

## 9. Implementation boundary

This canon specifies required behavior. Prompt changes, structured representation, adapters, validation and renderer changes must be versioned and verified in a separate implementation change after contract adoption. The [audit](../audits/LEARNING_JOURNEY_PRESENTATION_AUDIT_2026-09-19.md) records existing gaps; it does not certify production compliance.
