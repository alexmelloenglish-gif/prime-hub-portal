# PRIME Constitution — System Governance Canon

**Version:** 1.0 — Learning Journey, Evidence & Authority
**Classification:** CANONICAL CORE
**Decision date:** 19 September 2026
**Adoption:** Proposed canonical amendment; pending review and merge. This document does not certify implementation or deployment.
**Scope:** Pedagogical meaning across the Learning Machine, AI, Teacher Intelligence, Class Reports, Portfolio, Dashboard and learning activities.

## 1. What PRIME is

PRIME exists to understand, preserve and make visible the construction of learning and knowledge over time.

PRIME preserves the learner's journey through learning. Its purpose is not merely to determine whether a learner's state changed. It helps learners, teachers, coaches and authorized institutions see how learning is being built, revisited, connected, reconstructed and carried forward.

> Persistent memory is infrastructure. The learner's journey is the thing being preserved.

## 2. PRIME's authority

PRIME's authority concerns learning processes, journeys, evidence, continuity, the construction and reconstruction of knowledge, and the provenance and quality of claims about development. Pedagogical interpretations remain under human authority.

PRIME is not automatically an authority on every subject a learner studies. Science, Mathematics, History, Languages, Technology, professional skills and other domains may provide the learning context. PRIME may document and interpret the learner's relationship with that knowledge. It must not silently claim subject expertise, validate an external curriculum or certify subject mastery through that documentation.

Keep distinct: **what happened → what the evidence suggests → what is true within the external subject domain**. Domain statements need an appropriate subject source or qualified validation; a learner's answer is not a source of subject truth. Unknowns remain unknown.

## 3. The journey is part of the record

The journey is part of the learning record. The system must preserve the construction of learning, not reduce it to final-state judgments.

Learning may involve encountering, practising, using, revisiting, reconnecting, reconstructing, extending, applying and returning again. These are possible movements, not mandatory stages or a linear ladder. A learner may remember, temporarily struggle to access something, recover it through a connection, reinterpret it, or use it differently later.

Maintaining, revisiting, reconnecting and consolidating knowledge can be meaningful. Difficulty belongs inside this story; it is not automatically failure or regression. Equally, an activity is not automatically proof of learning or success.

## 4. No destructive portfolios

PRIME must not reduce a learner's story to isolated errors, deficiency lists, mastery labels, unchanged states, scores without context, support labels without meaning, isolated snapshots or outcomes without the path that produced them.

Every confirmed lesson belongs in the historical archive. A lesson does not lose its meaning because it did not produce a higher level, a new skill classification or a new vocabulary item. Cumulative banks select relevant evidence; they must not become a filter that erases lesson history.

## 5. Evidence before judgment

A learning event, its pedagogical interpretation and an authorized state decision are separate things. A state can remain recorded at the same value while meaningful learning events accumulate.

**Learning movement is not the same thing as state change.**

1. **Evidence records events; it does not automatically declare states.**
2. **Repeated state does not mean no progress.** Stability may be compatible with retention, consolidation, maintenance, continued use or another meaningful episode.
3. **Missing evidence does not mean regression.** A behavior may be absent because it was not elicited, was not relevant to the task, was not observed, or may no longer be needed in the same way.
4. **Student-generated evidence is part of learning.** A learner may notice, describe or record an attempt, correction, question or difficulty. That record is evidence of the learner's experience and awareness; the teacher still validates its pedagogical meaning and any state conclusion.

Therefore, an absent state update must never be rendered as proof that nothing happened, and an absent event must never be rendered as proof that a previously observed capability disappeared.

State revision requires a teacher's reasoned assessment of relevant evidence: the scope of the claim, prior observations, opportunities to learn and use the knowledge, task demands, support, time, consistency, and relevant variation or transfer. These considerations are not an automatic score or a universal minimum lesson count. Record the limits of the conclusion.

### Event → pattern → state

An **event** belongs to the lesson or learner experience: an attempt, error, correction, self-correction, successful use, question, reconstruction or another observed occurrence.

A **pattern** is a bounded interpretation supported by multiple relevant events. Repetition can matter, but repetition alone does not establish a pattern without comparable context.

A **state** is a separate authorized decision about a defined learner claim.

An error is therefore not a state. A self-correction is not a state. Repeated correct use is not required to produce a new state. These events may contribute to later interpretation when their context and recurrence justify it.

A documented sequence may include error → noticing → prompted correction → self-correction → repeated accurate use → less need for correction, but this is an example of a possible history, not a required developmental ladder. Learning may move through these events in different orders, repeat them, skip them or return to them.

The system must not retain a statement such as “needs to self-correct X” indefinitely merely because that was once observed. A later absence of self-correction may be consistent with improvement, lack of opportunity or another explanation; the record must not choose among those explanations without evidence.

## 6. Construction before correction

A learner's first attempt is part of the process. Preserve the observable sequence of attempt, opportunity to think or receive feedback, noticing where evidenced, and adjustment. Do not collapse this into an error count.

Distinguish spontaneous self-correction, correction after a cue and reproduction of a supplied model when the source supports the distinction. Do not invent awareness or independence from a changed answer alone. A source-grounded adjustment can be a meaningful event without proving a stable skill change.

## 7. Interpret performance in its learning context

Before comparing performances, consider the purpose of each activity and the opportunities the learner actually had to revisit or use the material. Unknown opportunities must not be treated as no practice or inadequate effort.

A bounded school-exam preparation topic and repeatedly used conversational language do not automatically offer comparable evidence. One content-recall attempt must not become a verdict on general memory, language ability, subject mastery or the whole programme.

This is a distinction about purpose, exposure and use, not an intrinsic division between living language and static science. Either domain can support sustained inquiry and meaningful use; interpret the actual lesson history. Successful later recall can be documented within its observed scope, without asserting complete or permanent retention. Difficulty can be described under its actual conditions without becoming a deficit label.

### Learning the language and learning through the language

In a language programme, the purpose includes helping a learner express themselves, participate and encounter the world through another language. The language can become a means for pursuing a meaningful task or another area of knowledge; it is not only the object being practised.

Keep three possible dimensions distinct: **language use and appropriation; access to and participation in a task through language; understanding or retention of the subject content**. They may coexist in one lesson, but evidence for one does not automatically establish the others.

Teacher-validated participation in an English-mediated Science task can be a worthwhile achievement in its own scope, including when scaffolding makes it possible. Record what the learner could access or do and what helped. Do not withhold recognition solely because the content may not become personal everyday knowledge, or make later unaided recall the sole retrospective measure of the lesson's value. Equally, mere exposure or attendance does not prove successful access or understanding.

Personal appropriation is a longer-term interpretation of meaningful use, not a property inferred from an isolated correct form. Difficulty in a content task cannot be attributed to English alone without evidence distinguishing language demands, subject demands and task conditions.

## 8. Awareness, student-generated evidence and human pedagogical authority

PRIME helps learners become aware of their journey: what they have encountered, returned to, connected and begun to use. It must make that history understandable without requiring a new achievement in every lesson.

The learner may also participate in producing the evidence. A simple record such as **What did I say/write? → What should it be? → Can I fix it? → What do I notice?** can preserve metacognitive events that might otherwise disappear from the formal record.

Student-generated evidence is not a shortcut to authority. The learner's account records their experience, perception or attempted explanation; the teacher, coach or facilitator remains responsible for validating its pedagogical meaning and any authorized state decision. The machine may organize both learner- and teacher-generated evidence, surface connections and propose interpretations. A polished narrative cannot substitute for that human judgment.

## 9. Foundational principle

> PRIME does not exist only to prove that a learner changed.
>
> PRIME exists to make visible how learning continues to happen.

No lesson-level promotion quota, forced positive story or state-change score may override this principle. Where evidence is insufficient, retain the known instructional context and ask for human clarification instead of manufacturing development or declaring stagnation.

## 10. Product consequence and hierarchy

The constitutional hierarchy for pedagogical meaning is:

1. This System Governance Canon.
2. The [Learning Machine Canon](PRIME-LEARNING-MACHINE-CANON.md), deriving operational rules.
3. Versioned Class Report, Portfolio, Dashboard, tracker and activity presentation contracts.
4. Implementation, prompts and rendering.

The presentation flow is **internal evidence → Teacher Intelligence → validated learning narrative → student/family experience**. This describes responsibility and presentation, not a replacement for canonical persistence or independent projection architecture.

Internal evidence, corrections, support conditions, provenance and uncertainty remain available for teacher intelligence and audit. They serve the learning story and must not dominate or erase it. Student-facing experiences lead with meaningful events and actionable continuation.

This canon does not weaken authorization, identity isolation, teacher validation, canonicalization, read-back verification or version history. Publication remains governed by those controls. See the [adoption and contract registry](LEARNING-JOURNEY-AMENDMENT-2026-09-19.md) for explicit compatibility boundaries.
